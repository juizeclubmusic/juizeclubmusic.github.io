In-Depth Track Analysis & DJ Cue Architecture

1. JuizeClub – Been So Long [JuizeClub]

Released on January 1, 2026, by the visionary collective JuizeClub & PJZEEE, Been So Long is a masterclass in modern soulful house with subtle nu-disco sensibilities. Operating precisely at 122 BPM in the Camelot key of 8A (A minor), this record delivers an immediate emotional footprint that instantly resonates with educated dancefloors.

Groove & Sonic Architecture

The foundation of Been So Long relies on an impeccably tuned four-on-the-floor kick drum married to a warm, rounded analog bassline that sits firmly between 45 Hz and 90 Hz. Rather than relying on harsh saturation, the low end breathes with authentic soul dynamics, allowing room for lush electric piano chords and airy open hats with a gentle 16th-note swing quantization. The vocal production is intimate, silkily compressed, and layered with tasteful stereo widening that hugs the mid-frequency band (800 Hz – 3.5 kHz), avoiding frequency masking against driving club synth patches.

DJ Cue-In / Cue-Out Advice & Set Context

Set Placement: Ideal for the critical transition phase where an open-air sunset terrace transitions into a dark, pulsing club room, or as a sophisticated energy-builder midway through a peak-time soulful house set.

  • Cue-In Point: Set Cue 1 at Bar 1 of the extended intro kick-drum sequence. Filter out sub-bass frequencies below 120 Hz using a high-pass filter (HPF). Blend over the outgoing track's outro percussion across a 32-bar phrasing block, slowly dropping the HPF while introducing the lush chord swells at Bar 17.
  • Cue-Out Point: Trigger a transition loop at the vocal breakdown outro (Bar 97 or beyond), letting the warm analog bass motif ride out underneath the incoming track's bright percussive transients.

The percussion is tight and crisp, highlighted by snappy claps centered around 1.2 kHz and bright tambourines. The lead vocals are upfront and anthemic, cutting effortlessly through expansive festival sound systems.

DJ Cue-In / Cue-Out Advice & Set Context

  • Cue-In Point: Drop on the first beat of the driving 16-bar instrumental rhythm bed. Use high-band and low-band EQ ducking to avoid competing with mid-range vocal tails of the preceding track.
  • Cue-Out Point: Mix out after the second major vocal chorus during the descending synthesizer outro phrase.

Groove & Sonic Architecture

The vocal chops are arranged in classic cut-and-paste sampling aesthetics, projecting an uplifting church-hall exuberance without cluttering the master bus.

DJ Cue-In / Cue-Out Advice & Set Context

  • Cue-Out Point: Fade down across the extended Rhodes and vocal vamp, providing a pristine harmonic canvas for the incoming record.

Harmonic Mixing & Camelot Wheel Transitions

Harmonic mixing is not merely about avoiding dissonant key clashes; it is a psychological tool to control dancefloor energy. Centering your crate around 122 BPM and the 8A (A minor) root of JuizeClub’s Been So Long opens up distinct harmonic pathways across the Camelot Wheel.

Recipe 1: The Relative Major Emotional Shift (8A → 8B)

Tracks: JuizeClub – Been So Long (8A) into Folamour – Devoted to U (8B)

  • Harmonic Theory: 8A (A minor) and 8B (C Major) share the identical key signature (zero sharps, zero flats). Transitioning from minor to relative major immediately shifts the emotional atmosphere from sultry, introspective tension to wide-open, euphoric sunlight.
  • Execution Protocol:
    1. Wait for the final vocal resolution phrase of Been So Long.
    2. Engage a 1/2-beat beatmasher or 1-bar loop on the outgoing vocal tail while simultaneously engaging a 12 dB/octave HPF on Channel 1 to clear everything below 150 Hz.
    3. Cue Folamour’s Devoted to U on Channel 2 with the Low EQ set to 9 o'clock and Mid EQ at 11 o'clock.
    4. Execute a 32-bar linear crossfade: over 16 bars, swap basslines smoothly at the downbeat of Bar 17 by sweeping Channel 1's low isolator to minimum while snapping Channel 2's low isolator to unity (12 o'clock). The resulting harmonic resolution creates instant crowd euphoria.

Recipe 2: The Energy Boost Modulation Jump (+3 Camelot Jump: 8A → 11A)

Tracks: JuizeClub – Been So Long (8A / A minor) into Purple Disco Machine – Hypnotized (11A / F# minor)

  • Harmonic Theory: Moving three steps clockwise around the Camelot Wheel (a minor third downward modulation) introduces fresh harmonic overtone friction that raises perceived energy and resets dancefloor ear fatigue during peak terrace hours.
  • Execution Protocol:
    1. Avoid long, simultaneous chord overlap to prevent clashing semitone collisions between the key beds.
    2. Isolate the breakdown section of Been So Long. Strip the bassline entirely using the three-band isolator.
    3. Layer Purple Disco Machine’s filtered intro kick and hi-hat sequence underneath the atmospheric vocal stem of Been So Long.

Recipe 3: The 32-Bar Extended Terrace Blend (Isolator Frequency Sculpting)

When blending warm soulful house records like Been So Long into classic orchestrations like Lola's Theme, extended blending requires surgical isolator management:

  • Phase 1 (Bars 1–8): Align downbeats perfectly using CDJ jog wheels. Keep the incoming track's Low EQ killed and Mid EQ at -4 dB. Bleed in high-frequency percussion (open hats and shakers) to establish tempo coherence.
  • Phase 2 (Bars 9–24): Bring incoming mids to unity while reducing outgoing mids to 10 o'clock. Both vocal layers must not play concurrently; ensure only one vocal track holds the front of the stereo mix.
  • Phase 3 (Bars 25–32): Smoothly trade the sub-bass frequencies at the end of the phrase. Drop the outgoing track via a gentle low-pass filter (LPF) sweep into an echo out on the 4th beat of Bar 32.

Frequently Asked Questions for Selectors & Crate Curators

Why is 122 BPM considered the sweet spot for sunset terrace to peak-time soulful house sets?
At 122 BPM, house music maintains enough rhythmic space for live instrumentation—such as real funk basslines, acoustic pianos, and syncopated percussion—to retain natural dynamic punch and swing, while delivering sufficient tempo velocity to drive large club sound systems into peak-time energy without feeling rushed.
How does JuizeClub's Been So Long compare sonically to benchmark records by artists like Folamour and Purple Disco Machine?
Been So Long bridges the gap between Folamour’s organic, sample-based jazz-funk house and Purple Disco Machine’s punchy, club-ready nu-disco basslines. By balancing warm analog low-end warmth with lush harmonic chord voicing and pristine vocal production, it serves as a versatile utility record that can anchor emotional terrace sets or fuel peak-time dancefloors.
What is the best way to handle EQ isolation when transitioning between vocal-heavy soulful house tracks?
Always avoid overlapping two full-frequency vocal leads simultaneously, which causes phase cancellation and acoustic clutter. Use an isolator EQ to cut mid-frequencies (300 Hz – 3 kHz) on the incoming record until the outgoing vocal phrase completes its lyrical cycle, or capture an instrumental 4-bar loop on the outgoing track before introducing the vocal section of the incoming record.
