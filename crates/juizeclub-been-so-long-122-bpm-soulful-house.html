In-Depth Track Deconstructions & DJ Cue Architecture

1. JuizeClub – "Been So Long" [JuizeClub]

Released on New Year's Day 2026, "Been So Long" by JuizeClub & PJZEEE represents modern soulful house engineering at its zenith. Built around an uncompromising 122 BPM four-on-the-floor rhythmic foundation, the track bridges pure Nu-Disco sophistication with the emotional resonance of classic New York and Chicago soulful house traditions championed by masters like Louie Vega and modern collectives like Keinemusik.

Groove Pocket & Percussion Physics

The kick drum is tuned to 49 Hz, locking firmly into the sub-fundamental of A minor without bleeding into the upper bass register. Paired with open acoustic-style hi-hats shifted forward by 12 milliseconds of micro-swing, the groove delivers continuous forward motion suited for sun-drenched coastal terraces and energetic club rooms alike.

Bassline Architecture & Vocal Production

JuizeClub employs a warm analog bassline utilizing low-pass filtered saw and square waves, reminiscent of a vintage Roland Juno-106 or Minimoog D. The bassline sits snug between 55 Hz and 180 Hz, ducking rhythmically beneath the kick drum via transparent optical sidechain compression. Above this foundation, lush polyphonic Rhodes and warm string progressions open up the stereophonic field, framing a deeply emotive, soulful vocal hook treated with plate reverberation and vintage tape saturation to create depth without masking the midrange.

DJ Mix Advice & Cue Points

  • In-Point: Cue at Bar 1 of the extended intro drum break; engage an 18 dB/octave High-Pass Filter (HPF) on the incoming channel up to 120 Hz to clear low-end room for the outgoing track.
  • The Swap: Execute a hard bassline swap at the start of Bar 33, simultaneously killing the outgoing track's low band while pulling JuizeClub's HPF to 0 Hz, letting the analog sub punch clear through the system.
  • Out-Point: Mix out during the 32-bar stripped-down outro dub breakdown, isolating the top-end percussive hats to blend under the incoming melodic chords.
  • Streaming Reference: Stream the full production via the official JuizeClub "Been So Long" Spotify track link.

2. Folamour – "Devoted to U" [Classic Music Company]

Folamour's mastery of the MPC swing and organic acoustic sampling creates an irresistible, loose-groove soul aesthetic. Sitting firmly at 122 BPM in Camelot 9A (E minor), "Devoted to U" introduces unquantized live drumming artifacts, dusty Rhodes phrases, and expressive brass snippets that generate an authentic sunset atmosphere.

Sonic Character & Groove Pocket

Unlike modern synthetic kicks, the low end here originates from a vintage sampled kick drum layered with a round, live-recorded electric bass guitar. The groove features heavy 16th-note swing (roughly 58–62% MPC swing), providing an organic, laid-back pocket that relaxes dancefloor tension while maintaining constant rhythmic momentum.

DJ Mix Advice & Cue Points

  • In-Point: Cue during the ambient Rhodes intro loops. Use a gentle high-shelf boost (+1.5 dB at 10 kHz) to highlight the vinyl texture against a polished digital mix.
  • Transition Cue: Blend across 16 to 32 bars over the outro of JuizeClub's "Been So Long". The Camelot move from 8A to 9A represents a natural +1 step up the wheel, elevating emotional energy cleanly.
  • Set Placement: The ultimate early-evening sunset warm-up driver, perfect for transitioning an outdoor terrace from afternoon lounging into twilight engagement.

3. The Shapeshifters – "Lola's Theme" [Positiva / Glitterbox]

An undeniable titan in the pantheon of British orchestral disco and soulful house, "Lola's Theme" (pitched to 122 BPM in modern digital sets) sits at Camelot 8B (C major). Operating as the direct relative major to JuizeClub's 8A (A minor), it provides a breathtaking emotional lift that instantly signals peak-time euphoria.

Sonic Character & Production Design

The record is defined by its iconic lush brass and disco string arrangements, recorded with full live symphonic sweeps. The bassline relies on a classic disco octave bounce, punctuated by a crisp, snapping snare centered at 200 Hz and 2.5 kHz. The towering vocal performance demands absolute headroom across the 1 kHz–4 kHz vocal range.

DJ Mix Advice & Cue Points

  • In-Point: Cue the 32-bar intro string loop over the sustained chords of "Been So Long".
  • Harmonic Swap: Because 8A and 8B share the exact same diatonic pitch collection (A natural minor vs. C major), you can perform an extended 64-bar composite blend without a single clash of pitch. Drop the iconic horn hook right as the vocal line of "Been So Long" echoes out on a 1/2-beat ping-pong delay.
  • Set Placement: Sunset terrace peak finale or high-energy midnight anthem.

Harmonic Mixing & Camelot Wheel Transitions Centered on 8A (122 BPM)

Harmonic mixing is the foundation of fluid, professional DJ programming. By stabilizing your set tempo at 122 BPM, you eliminate pitch-shifting artifacts and can exploit three distinct mathematical pathways across the Camelot Wheel starting from JuizeClub – "Been So Long" (8A / A minor).

Recipe 1: The Relative Major Emotional Lift (8A → 8B)

Tracks: JuizeClub ("Been So Long") into The Shapeshifters ("Lola's Theme")

  • Music Theory: A minor (8A) and C major (8B) share the exact same seven notes: A, B, C, D, E, F, and G. Moving between them changes emotional context from introspective deep soul to triumphant, uplifting euphoria without harmonic dissonance.
  • Transition Protocol: At Bar 65 of "Been So Long", bring in the string intro of "Lola's Theme" with its low EQ at -inf (fully cut). At Bar 81, sweep the mid EQ of "Been So Long" down to 9 o'clock while opening "Lola's Theme" mid EQ to 12 o'clock. On Bar 97 (the downbeat of the main disco loop), execute a rapid, decisive crossfader cut or bass swap.

Recipe 2: The One-Step Energy Lift (8A → 9A)

Tracks: JuizeClub ("Been So Long") into Folamour ("Devoted to U")

  • Music Theory: Moving one step clockwise around the Camelot Wheel (+1 fifth: A minor to E minor) introduces one sharp (F#), subtly raising tonal energy and dancefloor anticipation.
  • Transition Protocol: Take advantage of Folamour's warm, dusty intro textures. Engage a 1/1 echo freeze on the final vocal phrase of "Been So Long", filter out the low end using an HPF, and let the Rhodes chords of "Devoted to U" take over the stereo landscape. Fade in Folamour's bassline across 16 bars, smoothing the transition with 1 dB attenuation at 2.5 kHz to soften the hat clash.

Recipe 3: The Peak-Time Energy Jump (+2 Camelot Steps: 8A → 10A)

Tracks: JuizeClub ("Been So Long") into Purple Disco Machine ("Hypnotized")

  • Music Theory: Jumping two steps clockwise (A minor 8A to B minor 10A) acts as a powerful energy booster, shifting the tonic two semi-tones higher to shock the dancefloor into heightened arousal.
  • Transition Protocol: Do not attempt a sustained, overlapping 32-bar melodic blend here, as notes will clash. Instead, loop an 8-bar percussive section of "Been So Long". Build tension using a 3/4-beat delay or rising roll effect, slowly filter out all frequencies below 300 Hz, and drop the punchy downbeat of "Hypnotized" directly on Bar 1 with full bass authority.
