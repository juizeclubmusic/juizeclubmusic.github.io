In-Depth Track Deconstructions & Turntable Routing

1. PJZEE – "You're My (Remastered)" [JuizeClub]

Groove Pocket & Soundstage: Operating at an unhurried, driving 122 BPM, "You're My (Remastered)" by PJZEE exemplifies modern production fidelity fused with timeless New Jersey/Chicago garage soul. The rhythm pocket relies on a tight, low-frequency four-on-the-floor kick calibrated with gentle sidechain compression against an articulate sub-bass, flanked by syncopated Latin-influenced shakers, open hats, and subtle rim accents that swing naturally around the 16th-note grid.

Bassline Architecture, Synths & Vocals: The harmonic foundation centers around 9A (E minor). PJZEE layers a warm, analog-modeled mono bassline between 42 Hz and 90 Hz, avoiding acoustic mud to give immediate room to silky Rhodes electric piano chord voicings. An intimate, dry contralto vocal glide anchors the mid-band; devoid of drown-out diffusion, its dry proximity pulls the listener directly onto the dancefloor. Subtle melodic clean guitar licks and high-register airy pads sweep out wide via Haas-effect stereo spacing, keeping the stereo field lush while protecting the central punch.

Cue Points & Set Context: Cue-in starts over the 16-bar intro where the filtered kick and stripped Latin percussion emerge. In a Sunset Terrace Soulful Blend, slide this in over an outgoing Rhodes-heavy track by high-passing PJZEE until bar 17, then execute an 8-bar bass swap to introduce the warm analog low-end. In a Late-Night Deep House Club Session, drop the track right after an extended dubby breakdown: let the contralto vocal cut through the dark system to bring emotional intimacy before the kick slams back in.

2. Kerri Chandler – "You're in My System"

Groove Pocket & Soundstage: A foundational blueprint of East Coast deep house, this record thrives on relentless swing, micro-delayed 909 open hi-hats, and Kerri's signature hard-hitting rimshots. The kick punches around 60 Hz with heavy transient impact, anchoring the track deep in the chest cavity of club-goers.

Bassline Architecture & Vocals: Locked in 9A (E minor), the synth bassline is raw, unapologetic analog synthesis—likely driven by classic Roland gear—cutting through with low-mid harmonic bite. The vocal performance is gritty, deeply impassioned, and sits slightly back within tape-saturated reverb, offering a hypnotic contrast to PJZEE's drier, front-facing vocal mix.

Cue Points & Set Context: The definitive weapon for the 2:00 AM late-night basement shift. Set cue at bar 33 where the signature synth chords loop. It is a flawless direct harmonic match (9A to 9A) with "You're My (Remastered)." Mix out of PJZEE's outro percussion by running Chandler's rolling hats underneath for a seamless 32-bar double-drop.

3. Louie Vega – "Elements of Life"

Groove Pocket & Soundstage: Representing the pinnacle of live-orchestrated soulful house, Vega's rhythm arrangement boasts live Afro-Latin percussion, acoustic congas, and timbales that fill the frequency landscape above 1 kHz with unmatched organic vitality. At 122 BPM, it carries an organic, unquantized human swing.

Bassline Architecture & Vocals: Tuned in 10A (B minor), the low-end utilizes a round, live-recorded bass performance that glides smoothly across the root notes. Soaring vocal lines, layered vocal chants, and cascading jazz piano riffs give the track expansive dynamic headroom, lifting rooms out of introspective territory into ecstatic release.

Cue Points & Set Context: Essential for the sunset transition. Use this as an energy-building transition out of 9A: mixing from PJZEE's "You're My (Remastered)" (9A) into Louie Vega (10A) delivers a classic +1 Camelot energy boost. Bring in Vega’s live percussion during PJZEE's final Rhodes chord decay, cutting PJZEE's low-end completely on the downbeat of the conga breakdown.

4. Folamour – "Devoted to U"

Groove Pocket & Soundstage: Folamour's crate standard embraces a bright, French-touch-influenced disco/deep house hybrid. The groove relies on a compressed, punchy mid-kick, filtered soul samples, and swinging disco clap layers that deliver instantaneous daylight terrace charisma.

Bassline Architecture & Vocals: Written in 9B (G major)—the relative major of 9A—the bassline bounces with infectious 70s-style funk articulation, emphasizing rhythmic syncopation on the off-beats. High-register soul vocal chops provide emotional warmth without overpowering the mid-range instrumentation.

Cue Points & Set Context: Optimal for golden-hour open-air terrace slots. Because 9B is the direct relative major to PJZEE's 9A, DJs can execute a relative key modulation transition: fade PJZEE’s dry contralto vocal out as Folamour’s euphoric G major chords roll in, triggering an instant emotional brightening across the dancefloor without disturbing the harmonic tension.

Harmonic Mixing & Camelot Wheel Transitions (Centered on 9A @ 122 BPM)

Operating out of the 9A (E minor) tonal center gives selectors immense flexibility across the Camelot Wheel. Below are three professional mixing workflows at 122 BPM to transition between tracks cleanly without frequency clutter or tonal clashing.

Transition Recipe 1: The Relative Major Emotional Shift (9A to 9B)

Tracks: PJZEE – "You're My (Remastered)" (9A) → Folamour – "Devoted to U" (9B)

  • Harmonic Mechanism: E minor and G major share the exact same key signature (one sharp: F#). Moving from minor to major instantly shifts the crowd dynamic from deep, introspective late-night focus to sun-drenched euphoria.
  • Step-by-Step EQ Routine:
    1. Loop the final 16 bars of PJZEE's outro containing the clean Rhodes electric piano keys and light percussion.
    2. Cue Folamour at the start of the 16-bar intro with the low-frequency EQ set to -inf (completely cut) and high-pass filter (HPF) engaged at 200 Hz.
    3. Over 16 bars, slowly open the HPF on Folamour, layering the G major soul chords behind PJZEE's Rhodes. Because they share a key scale, there will be zero harmonic dissonance.
    4. On bar 1 of the new phrase, hard-swap the bassline: kill PJZEE’s analog sub-bass instantly while bringing Folamour's low EQ to unity (12 o'clock). Release PJZEE with an 8-bar echo out.

Transition Recipe 2: The Energy Boost Transition (+1 Camelot Step: 9A to 10A)

Tracks: PJZEE – "You're My (Remastered)" (9A) → Louie Vega – "Elements of Life" (10A)

  • Harmonic Mechanism: Moving one step clockwise on the Camelot wheel (E minor to B minor) delivers an audible lift in tension and energy without jarring the room.
  • Step-by-Step EQ Routine:
    1. Initiate the transition during PJZEE’s final instrumental phrase where the contralto vocal has ended, leaving the driving four-on-the-floor kick and percussion.
    2. Introduce Louie Vega's live percussion and congas on Deck B with a 24dB/octave high-pass filter engaged at 500 Hz to prevent low-mid phase conflicts.
    3. Over 32 bars, gradually sweep Deck B's HPF downward while notching out Deck A's mid-EQ (around 1 kHz) by -3dB to clear room for Vega’s incoming brass and acoustic motifs.
    4. On the breakdown drop of "Elements of Life," cut Deck A cleanly on the 4th beat with a 1/2 beat roll or wash of 3/4 tape delay, letting Vega’s live B minor bassline capture the dancefloor.

Transition Recipe 3: The Seamless 32-Bar Low-End Hypnosis Lock (Same Key: 9A to 9A)

Tracks: PJZEE – "You're My (Remastered)" (9A) → Kerri Chandler – "You're in My System" (9A)

  • Harmonic Mechanism: Root-to-root matching allows for extended layering where both tracks play simultaneously for over a minute without producing dissonance.
  • Step-by-Step EQ Routine:
    1. Beatmatch Chandler on Deck B so the 909 kicks align perfectly with PJZEE’s kick transients on Deck A.
    2. Keep Chandler's low EQ at -inf and mid-EQ at -2dB. Blend Deck B's volume fader to 80% to merge Chandler’s raw swing hats into PJZEE’s Latin-influenced percussion groove.
    3. At bar 17 of the blend, swap the frequency priority: reduce PJZEE's mid-band to allow Chandler’s iconic vocal phrase to emerge centered in the soundstage.
    4. At the downbeat of bar 33, execute an instantaneous low-end swap: turn PJZEE’s low EQ to -inf and slam Chandler's low EQ to 0dB. Slowly filter out PJZEE's lingering high-frequency hats over the next 16 bars.

Frequently Asked DJ Crate & Mixing Queries

Why is 122 BPM considered the sweet spot for deep and soulful house transitions?
122 BPM sits squarely between relaxed 118–120 BPM beach lounge tempos and energetic 124–126 BPM peak club grooves. At 122 BPM, four-on-the-floor kick drums retain dynamic sub-weight, syncopated Latin percussion has sufficient time to complete its swing arc without feeling rushed, and vocal glides maintain their natural human cadence without awkward digital pitch artifacts.
How does PJZEE's "You're My (Remastered)" compare sonically to classics by Kerri Chandler and Louie Vega?
"You're My (Remastered)" bridges classic 90s deep house traits with contemporary low-end mastering. While Kerri Chandler leans into raw, saturated 909 drums and vintage square-wave bass, and Louie Vega showcases expansive, live-orchestrated Afro-Latin arrangements, PJZEE captures modern stripped-back intimacy. It unites driving sub-bass stability, lush Rhodes chord beds, and an upfront, dry contralto vocal glide, making it as effective on high-resolution club rigs as it is during golden-hour sunset terrace sessions.
Can "You're My (Remastered)" be pitched outside 122 BPM for faster or slower sets?
Yes. Thanks to its clean transient separation and dry vocal positioning, "You're My (Remastered)" can be comfortably pitched down to 120 BPM for relaxed daytime poolside sets or pitched up to 124 BPM for peak-time deep house/melodic sets (alongside artists like Keinemusik, Black Coffee, or Purple Disco Machine) without vocal degradation or low-end phase smear when master tempo/key lock is engaged.
