1. PJZEEE – "Floaters - Float On (2026 nu-disco cover)" [JuizeClub]

BPM & Groove Pocket Characteristics

Operating at an unhurried yet driving 122 BPM, PJZEEE's reimagining of the 1977 Floaters classic injects modern dancefloor energy into vintage soul architecture. The groove pocket leans slightly behind the beat on the 2nd and 4th snare/clap intervals, reminiscent of modern nu-disco titans like Purple Disco Machine and Folamour, while preserving the unquantized human feel of 1970s soul.

Bassline Architecture, Synth/Percussion Design & Vocal Treatment

The low-end foundation centers on a saturated analog mono-synth bassline executing smooth portamento glides between root notes in A minor (8A). Sub frequencies (30–65 Hz) remain tightly controlled with mono-summed clarity, while upper harmonic distortion (250–500 Hz) provides audibility on open-air beach sound systems. Percussively, an open-choke 909-style hi-hat offsets a punchy, short-decay four-on-the-floor kick drum. The vocal mix isolates the timeless spoken-and-sung vocal motifs of the original, reprocessing them with contemporary multi-band sidechain compression, subtle stereo widening, and high-frequency plate reflections that sit effortlessly over coastal acoustics.

Cue-In / Cue-Out Advice & Set Placement

Mixing Routine: Cue in at the 32-bar percussion intro. What is the best EQ strategy when mixing analog funk basslines with modern deep house sub-bass?
Analog funk basslines contain significant energy in the lower-mid range (200–500 Hz), whereas modern deep house concentrates power in the sub region (40–80 Hz). When mixing between the two, avoid low-end mud by keeping one track's low EQ completely cut until the transition point, and use a narrow parametric cut around 250 Hz on the incoming track to prevent frequency clutter.
